Urad Dal Uttapam

Savory South Indian Pancakes

Thick, soft, and spongy savory pancakes made from a fermented rice and urad dal batter, generously topped with finely chopped vegetables.

Healthy Breakfast Veggie Packed Fermented Goodness

About Urad Dal Uttapam

Urad Dal Uttapam is a popular South Indian breakfast staple known for its pillowy texture and vibrant vegetable toppings. Made from the same fermented batter as dosa, it is poured thicker and cooked with onions, tomatoes, and chilies embedded in the surface.

Cuisine

South Indian

Serves

3–4 People

Cooking Time

15–20 Minutes
(excluding fermentation)

Ingredients

  • 2 cups fermented dosa/idli batter (rice and urad dal)
  • 1/2 cup finely chopped onions
  • 1/2 cup finely chopped tomatoes
  • 2 green chilies (minced)
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander (chopped)
  • Salt to taste
  • Oil or ghee for cooking
  • Chutney (for serving)

Preparation

  1. 1

    Prep Toppings

    Mix the chopped onions, tomatoes, chilies, and coriander in a bowl.

  2. 2

    Heat Tawa

    Heat a flat griddle or tawa and grease lightly with oil.

  3. 3

    Pour Batter

    Pour a ladleful of batter and spread it gently into a thick circle.

  4. 4

    Add Veggies

    Sprinkle a generous amount of the vegetable mix evenly on top.

  5. 5

    Press Gently

    Gently press the veggies into the wet batter with a spatula.

  6. 6

    Cook

    Drizzle oil around the edges, cover, and cook until the bottom is golden.

  7. 7

    Flip & Serve

    Flip to cook the other side slightly, then serve hot.

Perfect With

  • Coconut Chutney
  • Sambar
  • Tomato-Onion Chutney
  • Gunpowder (Podi)

Why It's Loved Worldwide

Uttapam is loved globally as a wholesome and customizable meal. Its thick, spongy base provides the perfect canvas for a variety of healthy vegetable toppings, making it a hit with both kids and adults.

Nutritional Benefits

  • Rich in Complex Carbohydrates
  • Good Source of Protein
  • Loaded with Vitamins (from veggies)
  • Fermented for Digestion
  • Low in Fat

Pro Tip

To ensure your Uttapam cooks perfectly without remaining raw in the center, always cook on a medium-low flame. Pouring the batter slightly thicker than a dosa allows it to become spongy, but cooking it covered for the first couple of minutes traps the steam and helps the batter and the vegetables cook thoroughly. For an extra punch of flavor, sprinkle a pinch of idli podi (gunpowder) over the wet batter before adding the vegetables.
